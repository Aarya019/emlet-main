## LinkedIn (2 posts)

**LinkedIn 1: the calendar**

```
You don't need an email for every date on the calendar.

A small list that hears from you twenty-five times a year, for twenty-five different reasons, learns to ignore you.

My rule of thumb for planning a year of email:

2. Keep only the ones where you have a real offer, event or story.
3. Work back from each date. Put the "start planning" day in your calendar, about four weeks ahead (six to eight for the big shopping dates).

**LinkedIn 2: subject lines on a phone**

```
Where your subject line gets cut off depends on the screen. Phones often show around 35 to 45 characters; desktop inboxes start trimming at roughly 60 to 70.

Which means the length matters less than the order.

This one buries the news:
"A quick note about something we've been working on: pre-orders open Friday"

This one leads with it:
"Pre-orders open: the refillable notebook"

Same news. One of them survives a phone.

Three habits that help:
- Lead with what the email is about
- Move dates and numbers forward
- Make the ending optional

## Reddit (2 posts, no links)

**Read first.** Subreddit rules differ and many ban or limit self-promotion. For every subreddit you post in:
- Read the sidebar rules and any "self-promotion" or "weekly thread" rule before posting.
- Use an account with some history. Brand-new accounts are often filtered.
- Do not put a link in the post. Answer the comments. If someone asks where to read more, you can link a blog post once, say you wrote it, and only if the rules allow it.
- Never post the same text in several subreddits.

Likely fits (check each one's current rules first): r/smallbusiness, r/emailmarketing, r/Entrepreneur, r/ecommerce.

**Reddit 1** (suggested title: *How do you decide which holidays are worth an email?*)

```
I'm planning a year of email for a small business and I'm trying to avoid the trap of emailing for every date on the calendar.

My rough rule: pick six to ten dates where there's a real offer, event or story, plan about four weeks ahead (more for Black Friday and Christmas), and leave the rest of the year for emails that aren't tied to a date, like a welcome email or a win-back.

How do you decide? Is there a date you always send for, or one you stopped bothering with?
```

**Reddit 2** (suggested title: *What do you actually send around Black Friday when you have a small list?*)

```
For a list of a few hundred to a few thousand, I keep coming back to three emails: a heads-up the Monday before with the offer and the dates, the main one on Friday morning, and a short last call on the final day to people who haven't clicked.

Fewer, clearer sends seem better than a daily barrage on a small list, but I'd like to hear what's worked for other people. Do you send more? Do you skip discounts altogether?
```

## Record shops: Record Store Day Black Friday (27 Nov)

This is the fastest outreach window in the whole plan. Record Store Day Black Friday is Friday 27 November, and shops
that take part have a reason to email their list this week.

Approach (build first, then message): make the shop an announcement email in its own colours in Emlet, take a screenshot,
then send the message. Ten a day at most. Prefer a DM, a contact form or in person to email. If you do email, use a
separate sending domain and mailbox, never `onboarding@emlet.app`. US business emails need your identity and a way to
opt out; the UK, EU and Canada are stricter, so check before emailing there.

```
Hi {first name},

I'm {your name}. I make Emlet, a tool that designs emails for independent shops.

I saw {shop} is taking part in Record Store Day Black Friday on the 27th, so I put together an announcement email in your colours: {screenshot or link}.

It's yours to use, free, no strings. If it's useful I can change the details. If not, no worries, and I won't follow up more than once.

{your name}
```

Replace `{...}` with real details for each shop. Do not claim a relationship you do not have, and do not send a generic
version without the screenshot.
