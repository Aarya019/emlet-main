# Marketing plan: 10 Nov 2026 to 10 Feb 2027

Written 2026-11-10. Content topics are detailed for the first two months (to 10 Jan); month three is an outline.
Everything about search demand below is **inferred from patterns and a handful of searches, not measured**. No search
volume data was available; see "Data that would sharpen this" at the end.

## Decisions so far

- **Not covering Lunar New Year or Ramadan/Eid** (decided 10 Nov). They are out of the calendar, the topic list and the template list.
- **Channels: X, Reddit and LinkedIn.**
- Hanukkah and Kwanzaa are only mentioned inside the inclusive "happy holidays" post, kept generic. Say if you would rather
  drop them too.
- Week 1 is under way: see the progress log at the end.

## 1. Reality check

- The site has 31 organic clicks in three months and half of them are the brand name (see `reference/seo/search-console-2026-10-baseline.md`).
  A three-month plan will not make seasonal search a big channel. What it can do is seed pages that rank later, give
  social and outreach something timely to say, and show which topics are worth doubling down on.
- Seasonal content for a new site needs lead time. Industry sources say 6 to 8 weeks minimum, and 10 to 12 weeks for a new
  blog (rules of thumb, not Google figures). Indexing is usually faster than ranking.
- So on 10 Nov the dates split into three groups:
  - **Too late for SEO this year (use for social and outreach only):** Black Friday, Small Business Saturday, Cyber Monday,
    Giving Tuesday, Record Store Day Black Friday, Hanukkah, Christmas. These pages are still worth keeping fresh because
    they will rank next season.
  - **Right at the minimum (publish this week and next):** January events, Valentine's Day, the Super Bowl.
  - **Comfortable:** Easter, International Women's Day, Mother's Day, Record Store Day (April).
- Biggest competitors for the head terms ("valentine's day email template"): the email platforms with template galleries
  (Beefree, GetResponse, Benchmark, Sender, Mailpro, Selzy, Mailmodo, Designmodo). A new site will not win those. It can win
  **narrow** ones: by industry ("valentine's email for a record store"), by style (Bauhaus, retro, handwritten), by
  occasion nobody covers well, and by honest "what to actually write" guidance.

## 5. Reusable event templates

**Yes, they fit what is already built.** The template library stores each template as one file, with a public preview,
subject ideas, "when to send" and "why it works" notes, and a "use in Emlet" flow that opens it in the visitor's own
brand colours. Event templates are the best use of it, because the same template is useful every year.

How they stay reusable:
- **Keep the URL free of the year.** `/templates/retro-valentines-bakery-offer`, not a 2027 version. Change the dates and
  the date field each season, keep the page, and keep its history.
- **Sample dates and placeholders** in the copy, and a note in "when to send" on what to replace.
- **Refresh 8 to 12 weeks before each event.**

Conditions:
1. **Occasion filing: built (week 1).** Templates now have an optional `occasion`, with `/templates/occasion/<key>` pages,
   index chips, a sitemap entry and a row on each template page. The registry already reserved the URL segment.
2. **Quality.** The library is hand-polished on purpose, because the generator still produces samey designs. Templates
   need to pass your "I would send this" check; the reference HTML and images you owe me raise the bar.
3. **Thin pages hurt.** A category page needs at least 3 templates to be indexed, and the nav stays off until about 6 are good.

## 6. Social posts and outreach (X, LinkedIn, Reddit)

Every blog post and template becomes 2 to 3 social posts (a template reveal, one tip from the post, a "this date is X
weeks away" nudge).

| Channel | Cadence | What works there | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 3 to 5 posts a week | Short tips, before-and-after examples, template preview images, one reply-bait question a week | Plain text plus the link, or the template preview image |
| LinkedIn | 1 to 2 posts a week | A practical how-to with a clear takeaway, the rule of thumb behind a blog post | Longer text, no hashtags needed |
| Reddit | 1 to 2 posts a week | Genuine questions and discussion; **no links in the post** | Text posts; reply to everyone; read each subreddit's self-promotion rules first |

Week 1 drafts for all three channels are in `reference/marketing/social/week-01.md`.

## 7. Other tactics to use

- **Update, do not duplicate.** One page per event, refreshed every year, beats a new page each year.
- **Link every new page** from at least two older ones (built into the process now).
- **Tie each post to a free tool.** The Subject Line Tester suits every subject-line post; the Footer Generator suits
  compliance posts; the Prompt Library has launch and event prompts.
- **Pinterest** for template previews, if you add it later: seasonal templates are the content type it suits.
- **Submit new URLs in Search Console** the day they go live.
- **A free printable "2027 email calendar"** as a download behind a signup is the one lead magnet worth building in the
  first month (the calendar post is the first step).

## 8. How to measure it

Because volumes are tiny, track leading indicators rather than totals:
- Every new post indexed within about 3 weeks (Search Console, Pages). If not, investigate before writing more.
- Impressions and average position for the event queries above, weekly.
- **Non-brand** clicks (everything except "emlet" and its typos): the number this work should move.
- Replies and comments on social posts, not likes.
- Template page visits, and signups by source (the signup-source tracking is in place).
- Reply and trial counts from outreach.

I will not put a traffic target on this; I do not have the data to forecast one honestly.

## 9. Data that would sharpen this

1. Google Trends (free) for these queries, last 5 years: "valentine's day email template", "new year email", "black
   friday email", "year in review email", "email marketing calendar", "super bowl email". It shows when interest starts
   to rise, which sets the real publish-by dates.
2. Keyword volume and difficulty for the same queries (Ahrefs Webmaster Tools or Google Keyword Planner).
3. Search Console, Performance, queries filtered to "valentine", "black friday", "new year", "holiday": what we already
   show up for.
4. The reference HTML templates and images you still owe, and the email types and seasonal occasions you most want
   (`reference/email-types.md`).
5. Your follower counts on X and LinkedIn, and whether your Reddit account has history.
